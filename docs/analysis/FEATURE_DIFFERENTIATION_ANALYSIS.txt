Pepforge: Feature Differentiation and Comparative Analysis
=========================================================

Pepforge is best understood as a workflow-oriented peptide research suite rather than a single-purpose peptide generator or synthesis calculator.

1. Compared with sequence-only analysis tools
--------------------------------------------

Many sequence tools provide residue properties, motifs, or conservation analysis. Pepforge includes a Hot Spot Finder, but its purpose is narrower and workflow-driven: it extracts design-relevant hotspot entries and prepares them for downstream peptide design. The GUI intentionally displays hotspot outputs rather than all intermediate data.

2. Compared with peptide design scripts
---------------------------------------

General peptide generators often produce sequences without synthesis-aware planning. Pepforge adds motif placement, bridge-style constraints, optional D-form/non-natural chemistry handling, terminal modifiers, labels, linkers, and ML-prior scoring controls. Chemistry features are explicitly toggled and hidden when disabled.

3. Compared with docking tools
------------------------------

Pepforge does not replace docking. Its ML-prior and hotspot scores are candidate-prioritization signals. Docking or structural validation should be used separately when structural interpretation is required.

4. Compared with spreadsheet-only SPPS planners
-----------------------------------------------

Spreadsheet templates are useful but can become difficult to maintain. Pepforge provides an editable SPPS plan generated directly from peptide notation, while still exporting CSV/XLSX files for Excel-based use. The table supports manual equivalents, repeat counts, catalysts, bases, solvents, and printable checklists.

5. Unique workflow strength
---------------------------

The distinctive value of Pepforge is the connection between:

    hotspot prioritization → candidate peptide design → SPPS planning → ML-ready logs

This creates a practical path from computational candidate selection to synthesis planning and future data accumulation.
